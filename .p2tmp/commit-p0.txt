perf(ops): measure the server's own simulation load (P0 of the multi-core work)

The live box sits at ~90% of ONE core with a full accept queue (Recv-Q 511/511),
and that CPU is the server's own simulation: every live field in a match steps
in lockstep at 30 Hz x GAME_SPEED (60 ticks per field-second), a tick costing
27-45 us (10/30/70 enemies, snapshot every 3) — i.e. 1.6-2.7 ms of CPU per
field-second, ~1.1%/field at the worst-case instant.

/healthz now reports the inputs, so the multi-core work can be sized from
production instead of guessed:
  fields      battles the server steps (FieldRunner.hosted, summed by
              lobby.stats(), plus a HeadlessPacer fast-forwarding a takeover)
  fieldsIdle  the subset whose match has no connected human (Match.liveHumans):
              a match nobody is watching still steps its takeover fields
  loop        event-loop delay p50/p99/max over a rolling <=10 s window
              (monitorEventLoopDelay; 10 ms resolution floors p50, so alarm on p99)

test/perf/hosted-load.mjs (manual, not part of node --test) reproduces the
numbers anywhere: one core carries ~370 hosted battles on average and ~90 at
peak, with nothing left for sockets, lobby or JSON. 2 vCPU caps a worker split
at ~2x, which is why suspending the fields of unwatched matches comes first.
